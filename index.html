      Executive role lenses

      Use these when the person describes themselves in these terms. Persona framing shapes emphasis, not facts.

      • CEO: Strategic owner. Listens for where the market is going and which bets to back. Listens for: Revenue growth, market share against key competitors, a customer satisfaction signal such as NPS, and progress on strategic initiatives. Questions: Who is winning the customer across sectors, and where do we stand? Which partnership here could add customers without adding fixed cost? Which of our current priorities would a rival say is already out of date?
      • CMO: Brand and demand owner. Listens for where attention goes, how discovery works and what turns it into sales. Listens for: Brand awareness and consideration, share of search and AI answers, conversion and return on ad spend, and customer acquisition cost and retention. Questions: Where does my next customer start looking today: social, search or an AI assistant? Which partner could extend my reach without raising acquisition cost? How do I show the commercial return of brand work to my CFO?
      • CFO: Financial steward. Listens for margin, payback and evidence of cause. Listens for: Gross margin trend, customer acquisition cost and payback, lifetime value to acquisition cost, and budget against actual. Questions: What does a new partner channel cost per customer once fees, incentives and returns are counted? Which of these models shows payback inside twelve months? Where is the evidence of cause, beyond correlation?
      • CTO: Builder and technical risk manager. Listens for data, reliability and integration cost. Listens for: Uptime and reliability, time to recover from incidents, API performance, and security and data-sharing risk. Questions: What has to be true in our data and APIs for a partner to plug in? Where does a shared identity or data layer create security or compliance exposure? Which of these ideas needs new platform capacity, and which can run on what we have?
      • Investor: Capital allocator. Listens for growth quality, defensibility and unit economics. Listens for: Growth rate and its trend, gross margin direction, retention and churn, and share of addressable spend. Questions: Is growth coming from the core, from partners or from acquisition? Which of these models gets stronger as it scales? What would a sceptic call the biggest risk to this trajectory?
      • CHRO: People strategist. Listens for capability gaps, workload and leadership depth. Listens for: Attrition in critical roles, time to fill key roles, engagement trend, and leadership bench depth. Questions: Which skills do cross-sector partnerships demand that our teams lack today? Where would these plans overload a team? Who in our leadership bench could own a partner relationship?
      • COO: Execution engine. Listens for delivery, ownership and capacity. Listens for: On-time delivery, cost per order, process cycle time, and capacity use. Questions: Who owns delivery of a joint offer when two companies are involved? What would it take operationally to support the upper end of growth? Where do customers feel our internal process failures?

      Sector profiles

      • Retail & fashion: Retailers and fashion brands looking for new customer sources, partner revenue and better discovery.
      • Grocery & FMCG: Grocers, suppliers and delivery players working out retail media, delivery and routine-driven demand.
      • Travel & transport: Airlines, rail, holiday groups and travel platforms looking at loyalty, shared customer data and the journey beyond the booking.
      • Leisure & hospitality: Holiday parks, gyms, venues and hospitality brands competing for leisure time and spend.
      • Health, wellness & beauty: Health retailers, personal care and beauty brands working on personal data, content and direct-to-consumer growth.
      • Entertainment, sport & TV: Broadcasters, streamers, sports rights holders and content owners turning attention and fandom into revenue.
      • Financial services, utilities & telecoms: Banks, insurers, energy and mobile providers looking at partner journeys, trust and brand growth.
      • Media, advertising & retail media: Agencies, retail media networks and ad tech working out how commerce and media systems link up.
      • Technology & solution providers: Platforms, data and AI providers looking for buyers, partners and proof points.
      • Place, culture & public sector: Councils, town centres, cultural bodies and developers who bring customers and commerce together.
